Meta-skill module

AI Fluency

Not a domain module — a meta-skill that sits on top of all of them. Every technique here is gated on already knowing where the evidence lives, which is what the other nine modules are for. This one is about getting an AI to help you find it instead of confidently pointing you at the wrong thing.

Nothing on these pages calls a model. The prompt comparisons are worked examples with the responses written out; the exercise is to run them yourself, against whatever AI tool you actually use, and compare what comes back.

The four competencies this module is organized around

Delegation
Deciding whether, when, and how to involve an AI at all.
Description
Describing the work well enough to get useful behavior back.
Discernment
Judging the output, the reasoning behind it, and the model's behavior in the session.
Diligence
Owning what you do with it — what goes in, what you disclose, what you vouch for.

They apply across three modes of interaction, and the mode changes what you owe in verification: Automation (it executes a task you defined), Augmentation (it advises, you decide and execute), and Agency (it acts on a system directly).

In practice ITOps lives almost entirely in the middle one — call it 90–95% Augmentation, where a tech brings a problem to an assistant and the session turns out to be some mix of research, discovery, remediation and documentation (the ticket, the wiki, or both). Automation and Agency are the minority by volume and the majority of the risk, which is why the Diligence leg spends most of its time there.

The four competencies and the three modes come from Anthropic's AI Fluency framework. What follows is that framework applied to infrastructure troubleshooting, which is not where its examples usually live.
