Wielding AI

An AI assistant is closer to an instrument than a tool. A screwdriver works the same in anyone's hand. An instrument doesn't — it gives back roughly what the player puts in, it has a range and tells you only learn by playing it, and getting good means practice, finesse, and deliberately pushing it to find where it runs out. This module treats the assistant as a partner you learn to play, not a vending machine you feed symptoms into.

It's a meta-skill, not a tenth domain. Every technique here is gated on already knowing where the evidence lives, which is what the other nine modules are for — the assistant can help you find that evidence faster, or confidently point you at the wrong thing, and the difference is mostly the player.

Nothing on these pages calls a model. The prompt comparisons are worked examples with the responses written out; the exercise is to run them yourself, against whatever assistant you actually use, and compare what comes back.

Running through all five is one question: who has hands on the keyboard? The assistant advises (you decide and make the change), it executes a task you defined exactly, or it acts on a system on its own judgment. The answer changes what you owe in checking and disclosure.

Where a tech starts, ITOps is almost entirely advisory — call it 90–95%, where a tech brings a problem to an assistant and the session turns out to be some mix of research, discovery, remediation and documentation (the ticket, the wiki, or both). That share doesn't stay put. It moves as the player grows with the instrument: one-off scripts turn into small reusable tools, and an agent starts showing up as a diagnostic instrument on a box you can afford to break. Executing and acting carry most of the risk whatever their share, which is why owning the result spends most of its time there — and why the share should grow on purpose rather than by drift.

Where this came from: the module started as notes on Anthropic's AI Fluency course, and some of its ideas trace back there. It has since been rebuilt around a different idea — the assistant as an instrument you practice with — and around ITOps specimens the course never covers, but the debt is real and worth stating.
